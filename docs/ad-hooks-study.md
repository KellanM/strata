# Ad hooks study: clickbait mechanics without the deception (2026-10-18)

Research only. No code or video was changed. Read this before making the next ad cut.

## Brief

Kellan asked: "Creative-study what it means to make effective clickbait ads and what would that look like for this app."

What is fixed:
- Strata ads use only real Strata gameplay, captured from the game.
- We borrow the hook mechanics, not the deception.

What can change: the hook, the structure, the text, the length, and which levels we show.

How we judge success: the ad stops the thumb in under 1 s; it reads with the sound off; a muted viewer could describe the game correctly afterwards; and players acquired by the ad stay as well as players acquired by the control ad.

**Baseline.** The v2 15 s ad (`trailer/v2/contact/ad-1..4.png` in the worktree). Frame 0 is the Maneki-neko block with five cubes left. "WHAT'S INSIDE?" slams in by 0.2 s and the reveal flash lands at about 1 s. Then the object, TAP / PEEL / REVEAL (1.25 s per word), and "100+ PUZZLES" over the wall at 9.6 s. "ONE TAP LEFT…" plays over the Dragon Head at 10.6 s, the payoff follows, and the end card (STRATA, PLAY NOW, store badges) comes at 12.8 s. It already does the first job well: motion and a question within 0.2 s. It has one hook type (curiosity), and two of its claims fail the honesty bar (see "Problems in our own ads").

### Rulings and policies (pages loaded unless marked [snippet])

- **ASA, Playrix, 30 Sep 2020** ([ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/plr-worldwide-sales-ltd-g20-1061644-plr-worldwide-sales-ltd.html)).
  - The ads: Homescapes, "Think you can do better?" / "Help her escape!"; Gardenscapes, "HOW TO LOOT?" / "ONLY 5% CAN SOLVE THIS". Both had the small print "Not all images represent actual gameplay".
  - The test was whether the content "was representative of the Homescapes and Gardenscapes games overall". The ruling found that players would have to play "a significant amount of content which was of a different style".
  - Playrix's own numbers: about 10 pin mini-games, one per roughly 20 levels, across 5,895 and 4,160 levels.
  - Breaches: CAP 3.1, 3.9 and 3.11. The "5%" line was not ruled on separately.
- **ASA, Evony, 3 Apr 2024** ([ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/top-games-inc-a23-1213924-top-games-inc.html)): the ad "did not reflect the game's core playing experience". *Core playing experience* is now the test.
- **ASA, Top War, Nov 2022** ([ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/rivergame-ltd-g22-1166092-rivergame-ltd.html)): real-looking footage still breached the code because "the speed of the combat … did not take place in the actual game". **Speed and physics exaggeration counts as misleading.** This matters for our speed ramps.
- **ASA, Gold and Goblins, Apr 2022** ([ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/appquantum-publishing-ltd-a21-1128337-appquantum-publishing-ltd.html)): the "cinematic representation" defence was rejected.
- **ASA guidance on app-game ads** ([page](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/entertainment-mobile-app-games.html)): ads must "be representative of the gameplay", and a disclaimer is unlikely to save a misleading ad.
- **Apple App Review Guidelines** ([page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)):
  - 2.3.1(a): misleading marketing "within or outside of the App Store" is grounds for removal.
  - 2.3.4: previews "may only use video screen captures of the app itself".
  - 2.3.7: no "unverifiable product claims".
- **Google Play Metadata policy** ([page](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842)): no misleading screenshots or videos; no "#1" or "best game ever" claims; no anonymous testimonials.
- **Google Ads Misrepresentation** ([page](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6020955)): bans "claims that entice the user with an improbable result", clickbait, and **unavailable offers**.
- **TikTok, Misleading and False Content** ([page](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-misleading-and-false-content)): bans exaggeration and ad/product mismatch.
- **TikTok playable ads** ([page](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/playable-ads)): "Be true to your app … Don't mislead users with gameplay that is inconsistent with your app."
- **Meta Advertising Standards, Unacceptable Business Practices** ([page](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/fraud-scams/unacceptable-business-practices/)): the page rendered thinly. The bans on "deceptive or exaggerated claims" and "sensational" content are from [snippets].
- **China CADPA game-ad standard, Feb 2025** [snippet] ([coverage](https://wnhub.io/news/marketing/item-47009)): reportedly bans non-existent gameplay.

### Practitioner and research evidence

- **Hook rate** = 3 s views ÷ impressions (TikTok often uses 2 s).
  - [Motion](https://motionapp.com/blog/key-creative-performance-metrics): under 25 % needs work; over 35 % is strong.
  - [Exactius](https://exacti.us/growth-library/thumb-stop-rate): the top decile reaches 35–45 %.
- **[TikTok](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/9-creative-tips-to-drive-auction-ad-performance):** ">63 % of the highest-CTR videos" show the key message or product in the first 3 s.
- **[Meta Instant Games creative docs](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/games/acquire/click-to-play-ads/creative):** 15–30 s, "the first 3 seconds are critical", don't open on a logo, "most users browse with sound muted".
- **Segwise Q4 2025 puzzle-ad report** ([report](https://mobile-game-creative-insights-report-q4-2025.segwise.ai/); 100+ ads sourced from AppMagic; vendor data, and internally inconsistent in places):
  - Format: 97 % are 9:16; 66 % open on gameplay; 62 % use a tap-hand.
  - Concepts: payoff reveal 46 %, fail bait 19 %.
  - Hook audio: ASMR 31 %.
  - Headlines: IQ/intelligence is the theme of 34 %.
  - CTA: "Play Now" is 67 % of text CTAs.
- **Segwise on Color Block Jam** ([post](https://segwise.ai/blog/color-block-jam-creative-strategy-breakdown.md)): clips cut "just before the solution"; real game sounds rather than music.
- **Segwise on Screwdom** ([post](https://segwise.ai/blog/screwdom-ad-strategy-creative-patterns.md)): 60 % of top ads used "Only 1% can solve this". A calm hook ("Let your mind rest") also performed.
- **Fedin, Game Developer 2022** ([article](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/big-mobile-marketing-creative-review-the-things-that-work)): noob-vs-pro means "a perfect walkthrough and a failed one". The fail must look like a real attempt, not a silly one.
- **Liftoff 2025 creative index** (via [RocketShip](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/?p=5467)):
  - Adding UGC raised impression-to-install by 152 % on average.
  - Playables convert 8–16× non-playables on impression-to-install.
- **Moon et al. 2026, *Psychology & Marketing*** (DOI 10.1002/mar.70130; [abstract](https://metatoc.com/papers/118177-fail-to-win-how-schadenfreude-and-competitive-traits-predict-download-intention-in-mobile-game-fail-ads)):
  - Fail ads work through schadenfreude, which predicted download intention in competitive people.
  - Failures at *moderate* difficulty produced more schadenfreude than failures at extreme difficulty.
- **Clark et al. 2009, *Neuron*** ([paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2658737/)): near-misses felt worse than misses but "increased desire to play". The effect appeared only when the subject had personal control.
- **Loewenstein 1994**: curiosity is the felt gap between what you know and what you want to know. It needs *some* prior knowledge to open.
- **Ghibellini & Meier 2025 meta-analysis** ([paper](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v12y2025i1d10.1057_s41599-025-05000-w.html)): unfinished tasks are **not** remembered better (no Zeigarnik effect), but people do feel an urge to *resume* them (the Ovsiankina effect). "Cut before the solve" works through the urge to finish, not through memory.
- **Poerio et al. 2018, *PLOS ONE*** ([paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6010208)): ASMR lowered heart rate by about 3 bpm in people who respond to it. There is no direct research on "oddly satisfying" videos.
- **Consequences of misleading ads:**
  - [Nexters survey 2023](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/nexters-misleading-ads-survey-2023): n = 5,212; 91 % of US players notice misleading ads; about 75 % say the ads look more fun than the games.
  - [Mistplay](https://business.mistplay.com/resources/false-advertising-in-mobile-games/): 36 % say the ad must match the game for them to stay.
  - Segwise claims D1 retention of 32 % for honest ads vs 14 % for fake ones ([post](https://segwise.ai/blog/fake-ads-and-mobile-gaming-how-studios-balance-clickbait-gameplay-integrity-and-profitability-cm7j2hc9s00bvip0l0r4k59lw)). **No methodology or data are shown, so treat it as unverified.**
  - [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2020/10/13/how-playrix-township-became-a-billion-dollar-game) says fake ads "are not performing great in terms of retention", and that Playrix later "de-faked" them by building the mini-games into the games.
  - Why studios do it anyway: CPI. [Seufert, Mobile Dev Memo](https://mdm.co/why-do-mobile-gaming-companies-use-fake-ads): "no advertiser really knows why an ad works".
  - I found **no primary, published retention study**. Our own D1 numbers will be the evidence.

## Mechanisms: why each hook stops the thumb

| Hook type | Mechanism | Where it works | Where it fails | Honesty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Curiosity / "what's inside"** | Information gap (Loewenstein). The covered object is the gap; the reveal closes it. | When the viewer has enough to guess: a recognisable silhouette, a shape that is half-there. | A blank cube with no partial shape gives too little to wonder about. | OK. It's our core loop. |
| **Fail / "almost"** | Competence itch: "I'd have done better" (Moon et al.: schadenfreude → intent). Near-miss: so close that you want to act. | Moderate difficulty, where the error looks like a real attempt (Fedin). | An absurd fail reads as fake and invites "rage installs" that churn. | OK **only if the fail is a real move in the real game**. A scripted fail in a mode that doesn't exist is the Playrix case. |
| **Challenge / "only X% can"** | Status threat plus self-selection: "Am I in the X %?" | Short puzzles where the viewer can try in their head. | When the number is invented. | **A made-up percentage is deceptive** (CAP 3.7 substantiation, Google "improbable result", Apple 2.3.7). A *question* ("Can you do it with zero ✕?") or a *verifiable fact* ("the hardest level in Strata") is OK. |
| **Satisfying / ASMR** | Rhythmic, predictable destruction with rising pitch: a low-effort reward to watch, and it lowers arousal (Poerio). | Dense, continuous action with sound. Muted, it must still look tactile. | Static frames; or a speed-up that makes the game look faster than it plays. | OK if the speed is honest. **Top War ruling: exaggerated speed is misleading.** |
| **Can you spot it / guess** | Active participation: the viewer is *playing* during the ad. | A countdown with enough clues to guess, then the answer. | When the answer is unguessable or the countdown is too long. | OK. |
| **Cliffhanger / cut before the solve** | The urge to resume (Ovsiankina) rather than memory. | The 2–3 s before the payoff. | If it never pays off, it can feel like bait. Kellan asked the v2 ad to pay off. | OK when the cliffhanger is real. Keep one payoff on screen. |
| **Wrong move → correction** | Near-miss plus the competence itch, then relief. It teaches the rule in passing. | When the rule is visible: the clue says this cube stays. | A comedy-dumb error. | OK. Strata has a real mistake state (below). |
| **Progression / level 1 vs N** | Aspiration, and proof of depth: "it grows". | Both levels real and shown in order. | Gardenscapes' "lv 1 vs lv 55" CGI soap. | OK when both are real level captures and the level numbers are true. |
| **Before / after, comparison** | Contrast in one frame. The brain reads the difference without reading text. | Split screen, same framing. | When the "before" is a strawman. | OK. |
| **UGC / reaction** | Social proof plus a human face as a pattern interrupt. Liftoff: +152 % IPM. | Genuine players and reactions; a paid partnership disclosed. | Scripted fake testimonials ("Sarah ★★★★★"); "my bestie is playing". | **Fake testimonials are off-limits** (Google Play bans anonymous testimonials; FTC/CMA endorsement rules). Real creators with disclosure are OK. |
| **Pattern interrupt** | Something unexpected in the feed: live hands, skin, a tornado. | Must cut to the product within about 3 s. | When it is unrelated to the game (bait-and-switch). | OK when it is ours, e.g. real hands holding a phone playing Strata. Not OK when it is footage from something else. |
| **Playable** | You try it in the ad. Liftoff reports 8–16× impression-to-install. | Single mechanic, under 20 s, ends on a high. | Too much tutorial. | OK (TikTok: "Be true to your app"). |

### What is off-limits for Strata

1. Footage that isn't captured from Strata: CGI, cinematics, other games, or mocked boards.
2. Mechanics Strata doesn't have: falling pieces, pin-pulls, characters in peril.
3. Invented statistics: "only 1 %/2 % can solve", "99 % fail". We have **no completion analytics**, so any percentage is unsubstantiated today.
4. Superlatives we can't prove: "hardest puzzle game in the world", "#1", "best game of 2026", "featured on…".
5. Fake testimonials, fake UGC, fake "my bestie" voices.
6. **Speed that misrepresents play.** The v2 cuts rush game time 1.8–4× (picture only) to land reveals before a cut. The ASA's Top War ruling makes this the subtle risk. Keep rushes to the *animation* after the last tap: the reveal, gems and spin. Never speed up the chiselling itself, or disclose it with a visible "×4" marker.
7. Offers that don't exist yet. The store badges and PLAY NOW pointing to stores that don't list Strata breach Google's "unavailable offers" rule. Until listings exist, the CTA must be "Play free in your browser".
8. Disclaimers as a fix. Per the ASA, small print does not cure a misleading ad.

### Problems in our own ads (found in this study)

- **"100+ PUZZLES" is false today.** `shapes.js` has 67 main levels, 5 bonus shrines and 5 tutorial lessons: 72 hand-made objects, or 77 counting lessons. The random-shape generator (`startRandom`) makes endless blobs, but counting those under a wall of hand-made objects would mislead. **Change to "70+ PUZZLES"** (or the exact count) in both cuts, or add levels before the ad runs.
- **The store badges are aspirational** (already noted in trailer-v2-study). They must not run until Strata is listed.
- The 1.8–4× picture-only rushes (point 6 above). The current cuts rush the post-tap reveal, which is defensible, but the rule should be written down.

## Strata facts the concepts rely on (from the code)

  - 7×6×7: Mushroom and Pumpkin.
  - 7×8×7: Palm Islet and Wishing Well.

## Ad concepts for Strata

All footage is real capture. Times are in screen seconds. Text is in the game's letterspaced caps unless marked otherwise.

### 1. "Don't tap that one" (wrong move → correction)

- **Format:** 10 s.
- **First frame:** the Origami Fox block (D 14.4, so the viewer believes the error) at zoom 1.3. About 8 cubes are left, and a finger hovers over a cube on the fox's face. The text "DON'T TAP THAT ONE" is already sharp on frame 0.
- **Storyboard:**
  - 0.0–0.8: the finger lands. The cube flashes red and shudders, and ✕ appears.
  - 0.8–1.6: the text swaps to "IT'S PART OF THE FOX". A slow push-in on the red cube, which is now marked.
  - 1.6–4.5: the finger taps the right cubes, 6 taps rising in pitch.
  - 4.5–5.2: the last tap, flash and reveal.
  - 5.2–8.0: the fox leaps and turns to camera; 2 gems land, and the empty third gem slot shows.
  - 8.0–10: end card with "ZERO ✕ = ★★★".
- **Why it stops the thumb:**
  - A red error in a calm palette is a pattern interrupt.
  - It creates the competence itch: "I wouldn't have."
  - The missing third gem is a near-miss that you want to fix yourself.
- **Risk:** it reads as punishing, which is off-tone for a calm game. Mitigate by making the voice wry, not mocking.
- **A/B:**
  - Test the hook text: "DON'T TAP THAT ONE" vs "WRONG CUBE" vs no text.
  - Test the first frame: finger mid-air vs red flash already happening.

### 2. "Same block. Different secret." (guess / spot it)

- **Format:** 12 s.
- **First frame:** three identical 5×10×5 blocks side by side in thirds, each mid-chisel, with "SAME BLOCK" on top.
- **Storyboard:**
  - 0–1: "SAME BLOCK".
  - 1–2.2: "3 SECRETS" while all three chisel in sync.
  - 2.2–6: the cubes fall and the silhouettes diverge (a tapering cone, a fin, a hat brim). "GUESS?" pulses at 4 s.
  - 6–9: the reveals one at a time, left to right: Garden Gnome, Lighthouse, Rocket, each with its title.
  - 9–12: "70+ SECRETS" over the content wall, then the end card.
- **Capture:** Garden Gnome, Lighthouse and Rocket from the same camera, plus the grid.
- **Why it stops the thumb:** three identical towers is an odd image in a feed. It invites guessing (active participation), and the payoff comes three times.
- **Risk:** the three-up composition is small on a phone. Mitigate with zoom and a tall crop: each third is 360×1920, which suits towers.
- **A/B:**
  - Test the Gnome/Lighthouse/Rocket trio vs the 5×9×5 quartet (Pine, Spiral, Kokeshi, Snowman).
  - Test "GUESS?" vs "WHICH IS THE ROCKET?".

### 3. "Level 1 vs Level 67" (progression)

- **Format:** 10 s, split screen.
- **First frame:** the top half is Steps (48 cubes) labelled "LEVEL 1"; the bottom half is Observatory (343 cubes) labelled "LEVEL 67". Both are mid-chisel.
- **Storyboard:**
  - 0–2: Steps finishes. Its reveal comes at 1.5 s while the Observatory is still a wall of numbers.
  - 2–6: the camera stays on the Observatory as layers are peeled and cubes fall in long runs.
  - 6–8: the Observatory reveal fills the frame, wiping the split, and the dome turns.
  - 8–10: "67 LEVELS · 7 WORLDS", then the end card.
- **Why it stops the thumb:**
  - The split contrast reads instantly.
  - The hard puzzle shows depth to someone who thinks puzzle games are trivial.
  - The easy puzzle shows they can start.
- **Risk:** the Observatory capture compresses a long solve. Don't rush the chiselling (off-limits point 6). Cut between real segments with visible whips instead.
- **A/B:**
  - Test numbers vs world names ("FIRST LIGHT vs STARFALL").
  - Test which half reveals first.

### 4. "This block is hiding a dragon" (declared payoff, reverse curiosity)

- **Format:** 8 s.
- **First frame:** the full Dragon Head block, with "THIS BLOCK IS HIDING A DRAGON".
- **Storyboard:**
  - 0–1: the text, with the first crack at 0.3 s.
  - 1–4.5: the chisel run. Green and red face colours show through the gaps as the solid cubes paint.
  - 4.5–5.5: "ONE TAP LEFT…", then the tap.
  - 5.5–8: the head turns to us, the gems land, then the end card.
- **Why it stops the thumb:** a concrete claim beats an abstract question. The viewer checks the claim: "where's the dragon?" (curiosity with prior knowledge, per Loewenstein).
- **Risk:** naming the payoff removes surprise. Test it against the baseline "WHAT'S INSIDE?".
- **A/B:**
  - Test "dragon" vs "cat" (Maneki-neko) vs "fox".
  - Test this against the baseline.

### 5. "Wait for it" (honest fake-out)

- **Format:** 8 s.
- **First frame:** the Mushroom block (7×6×7) about 70 % chiselled. A dome shape is visible, with "EASY. IT'S A MUSHROOM."
- **Storyboard:**
  - 0–2: the dome sharpens.
  - 2–3: "…RIGHT?"
  - 3–5: the final taps.
  - 5–8: the reveal of the real level, then the end card.
- **Capture:** the honest version needs a pair whose midpoints really look alike. Pumpkin and Mushroom share a 7×6×7 block. Show the *Pumpkin* level with the caption "a mushroom?" and let it resolve as a pumpkin.
- **Why it stops the thumb:** expectation violation plus a mini-guess.
- **Risk:** the midpoints may not look alike. **This needs a capture test first:** run `prechisel` at 40/60/80 % on Pumpkin and look at the contact sheet. If no frame is ambiguous, drop the concept.
- **A/B:** test this fake-out vs a straight "guess" (concept 6).

### 6. "Guess in 3" (countdown)

- **Format:** 9 s.
- **First frame:** a big "3" over a half-chiselled Snail block.
- **Storyboard:**
  - 0–3: "3… 2… 1…" with cubes falling on each beat and the silhouette sharpening.
  - 3–4: "SNAIL!", the reveal and the spin.
  - 4–7: a quick second round on Pelican with no text: 2 beats, then the reveal.
  - 7–9: "CAN YOU GUESS FASTER?", then the end card.
- **Why it stops the thumb:** the countdown is a timer the viewer races. It's an active game inside the ad.
- **Risk:** guessing too early makes it boring; too late is frustrating. Tune how much of the shape shows at the "3".
- **A/B:** test Snail vs Teapot vs Lighthouse as the round-one object; test 3 rounds vs 2.

### 7. "Turn your sound on" (ASMR chisel run)

- **Format:** 15 s.
- **First frame:** the Tea House (504 cubes) at a tight zoom, with a run already mid-cascade and fragments in the air. A small 🔊 sits in the corner. No headline for the first 2 s.
- **Storyboard:**
  - 0–8: one long, *real-time* chisel run. Notes climb the pentatonic scale, fragments burst on every tap, and the camera slowly orbits.
  - 8–10: peel a layer.
  - 10–12: the reveal.
  - 12–15: "ONE MORE?", then the end card.
- **Why it stops the thumb:** continuous tactile destruction (the "satisfying" format; ASMR is 31 % of puzzle hook audio). Muted, the fragments and rising rays still read.
- **Risk:** without sound it may be the weakest muted hook. The Tea House reveal may read small.
- **A/B:** test Tea House vs Wishing Well (392 cubes, whose reveal is proven in v1); test with the 🔊 vs without.

### 8. "Before / After" (comparison)

- **Format:** 8 s.
- **First frame:** a 2×2 grid of four untouched blocks (Kokeshi, Maneki-neko, Koinobori, Daruma) with "BEFORE".
- **Storyboard:**
  - 0–1.5: "BEFORE".
  - 1.5–4: the four blocks chisel in sync, with whip cuts.
  - 4–6: "AFTER" as the four objects reveal at once and spin.
  - 6–8: "70+ OBJECTS", then the end card.
- **Why it stops the thumb:** the grid of identical stone cubes is visually odd, and the contrast lands with no reading.
- **Risk:** it overlaps with concept 2. Prefer 2 if both test the same.
- **A/B:** test one world vs a mixed set; test a 2×2 grid vs a single large block.

### 9. "The hardest one in Strata" (honest challenge)

- **Format:** 12 s.
- **First frame:** the Observatory block whole, with "THE HARDEST PUZZLE IN STRATA". This is true by our difficulty metric, and it is the last level.
- **Storyboard:**
  - 0–1.5: the hook text.
  - 1.5–3: "343 CUBES. NO GUESSING."
  - 3–8: real chisel and peel segments with whip cuts and no speed-up. A clue line is shown solving, then the next.
  - 8–10: the reveal.
  - 10–12: "CAN YOU DO IT WITH ZERO ✕?", then the end card.
- **Why it stops the thumb:** the challenge frame without a fake percentage. The status threat is "is it hard for *me*?"
- **Risk:** it may scare off casual players, so test it against concept 3. "No guessing" is true: every level is line-solvable (`levels.mjs` "ok"). Keep that check in the build.
- **A/B:** test "HARDEST IN STRATA" vs "CAN YOU SOLVE THIS?"; test Observatory vs Spiral Stair.

### 10. "Real hands" (UGC framing with a real player)

- **Format:** 15 s.
- **First frame:** live-action, over the shoulder. A thumb taps a phone showing Strata mid-solve. Text: "my 10-minute break".
- **Storyboard:**
  - 0–5: a real player solving the Moon Rabbit. The phone screen is a direct screen capture, composited at full resolution.
  - 5–8: the reveal on the phone and a small genuine reaction.
  - 8–12: a slow, "calm" voice-over or captions.
  - 12–15: the end card.
- **Why it stops the thumb:** a human face or hands is the strongest pattern interrupt in the feed (Liftoff: +152 % IPM for UGC). The calm tone fits Strata; the "only 1 %" tone does not.
- **Risk:** it needs filming, which the pipeline can't do. Any creator must be **real and disclosed** (#ad); no scripted "Sarah ★★★★★".
- **A/B:** test hands-only vs a face; test a calm caption vs a "this is so satisfying" reaction.

### 11. Playable (later)

- **Format:** one HTML file running a single small real level (Heart or Snail, 7×6×2 / 8×6×2).
- **Flow:** about 6 taps with a tap-hand, then the reveal, then the end card.
- **Why:** Strata is already a three.js web app, so a trimmed build is plausible. Networks cap playables at about 2–5 MB as a single HTML file; that needs checking per network.
- **Honesty:** the most honest format there is: it *is* the game.
- **Cost:** high. Do it after video winners are known.

## Top 3 to produce first

1. **#1 "Don't tap that one."**
   - **Why first:** the fail/near-miss hook is the strongest documented mechanism (Moon et al.; Fedin; 19 % of puzzle ads). Strata can do it 100 % honestly, because the mistake state is real. It also teaches the core rule and the stars.
   - **Build:**
     - Add `H.wrongTap()` in `page.mjs`. It finds a visible solid cube via the existing `toGo`/`pickAt` logic and calls the game's own tap path, not a fake flash.
     - The script is `level('origami-fox', 1.3, {left: 8})`, `finger`, `wrongTap`, `text`, then 7 × `tap`, `flash`, `turn` and `endcard`.
     - The audio pass picks up the real `sound.mistake()` thud for free.
2. **#2 "Same block. Different secret."**
   - **Why:** it shows abundance and guessability at once, and it is unique to a 3D nonogram. No competitor I saw can show this.
   - **Build:**
     - Run three captures with an identical script and camera, one per level (gnome, lighthouse, rocket). Each is a 360-wide crop, or render the page at 360×1920.
     - Add an `hstack` composite in `build.mjs`. Text goes on top in a final overlay pass, or one capture can carry it.
     - Mix the audio from one capture, so the three runs don't play three chisel melodies at once.
     - Stagger the solves so the reveals come one at a time.
3. **#3 "Level 1 vs Level 67."**
   - **Why:** the progression and power-fantasy format, using the level numbers that are really in the game.
   - **Build:**
     - Two captures (Steps and Observatory) and a `vstack` composite for 0–6 s.
     - Then the full-frame Observatory capture for the reveal.
     - Long-solve segments are joined with whips, not speed ramps.

**Before any of these run, fix the baseline's honesty problems:** change "100+" to "70+", and switch the CTA to "Play free in your browser" until the store listings exist. Keep the current "WHAT'S INSIDE?" ad as the **control** in every test.

## Testing plan

**Setup.**
- Build 6–8 s *hook cuts*: the same 5 s body (reveal, wall, end card) with different first 3 s.
- Test hooks first, then extend the winners to 15 s.
- Run on one platform (Meta or TikTok), with one ad set per test and equal budget per creative.
- Each creative links with its own `utm_content` to the PWA.

**Metrics, in funnel order:**

| Metric | Definition | Read |
|---|---|---|
| Hook rate (thumb-stop) | 3 s views ÷ impressions (TikTok 2 s) | < 25 % weak, > 35 % strong. About 1,250 impressions per arm detects a 25 → 30 % difference at 80 % power. |
| Hold rate | 75 % views or ThruPlays ÷ 3 s views | Separates "stopped" from "stayed". |
| CTR | link clicks ÷ impressions | Interest after the hook. |
| Cost per play-start | spend ÷ PWA sessions that start a level | Our CPI substitute while web-only. |
| **D1 return** (honesty check) | % of a creative's players who come back the next day | **The guardrail.** A hook that wins CTR but loses D1 or first-level completion against the control is over-promising: revise or kill it. |
| First-session promise check | % who finish their first level; mistakes in it | Shows whether the ad set the right expectation (e.g. #1 viewers should make fewer ✕). |

**Prerequisite:** the PWA has **no analytics today**. Before any paid test, add minimal, privacy-respecting events:
- session start, with `utm_content`;
- level start;
- level solved;
- return visit on a later day.

That data also unlocks the only honest "X % finish this" claims later: real completion rates for real levels.

**Decision rule:**
1. Rank by hook rate, then CTR.
2. A creative is a winner only if its D1 and first-level completion are not worse than the control.
3. D1 needs about 1,000+ players per arm to detect a 5-point difference, so treat it as a directional guardrail at small budgets.

## Validation limits

- I inspected sampled frames, not full-speed playback, and heard no audio. Ad descriptions are of picture only, at 240–360p.
- Practitioner percentages come from vendors with a commercial interest. The 32 % vs 14 % D1 figure is unverified.
- None of the Strata concepts has been captured. Concept 5 depends on a capture test that may fail.
- Nothing here has been tested with an audience. The first real evidence will come from the hook-cut test above.
