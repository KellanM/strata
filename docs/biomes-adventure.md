# Chapter II: from Teatime to the first steps of the journey

**Proposal, 2026-10-18. Nothing is applied:** `CHAPTERS` and the levels are
unchanged until Kellan picks a concept and candidates. Gallery page 10 shows
the top four concepts with 42 candidates.

> "The idea of tea time isn't the most captivating if the idea of this game
> is going on a grand adventure."

## In short

- **Recommendation: I · The Waymarks**, the old road out of the valley,
  marked by those who walked it first. It is the trail that First Light's
  stones already promise. Beacon Hills is the runner-up.
- **Teatime's nine stones:**
  - Teabag Pal, Toaster, Fried Egg and Teapot go back to First Light. All
    four lived there until today.
  - Bunny in a Teacup goes to Moss Garden.
  - Mug goes back to The Lagoon, beside the lighthouse, since its story
    already names "the keeper".
  - Stand Mixer goes to Clockwork Workshop.
  - Cake Stand and the Layer Cake shrine shipped today and have no place on
    the road, so they become gallery-only.
- **To keep returning players safe,** keep ids when moving levels, list the
  new chapter's level ids in `ADDED_LATER`, and never reuse a retired id
  (details below).

## The slot

Chapter II sits between **First Light** (home and morning) and **Moss
Garden** (small creatures of the garden). It is the threshold: the first
steps away from home. So it needs:

- **Easy, small, simple objects.** The level mean must land between First
  Light and Moss Garden. Today that is 7.67 to 9.03 D. If Teatime's
  levels move as proposed below, it becomes 7.95 to 9.21 D.
- **A sky distinct from both neighbours:**
  - First Light (Dawn): peach to mauve, `#f7dccb → #c6a3c4`.
  - Moss Garden (Mint): pale green to sage, `#eaf2df → #aed0c0`.

  So no peach, mauve or green.
- **A clear island on the new cloud-sea map.** One peak that says what the
  place is, with the golden path running over it.
- **The tone of Journey, Monument Valley and Shadow of the Colossus:**
  mythic, vast, quiet, a call outward. It should still be gentle enough
  for chapter II.

### I · The Waymarks

- **Mood.** The old road out of the valley, marked by the people who walked
  it first: cairns, posts, a lamp left lit.
- **Sky.** `#d5ddf1 → #aebfe3`, a cool morning periwinkle. The road's
  materials are warm (stone, wood, leather, brass), so a cool sky makes them
  stand out. A gold sky swallowed them in tests.
- **Why here.** First Light already sets up the road:
  - the Key "fits a lock somewhere further down the trail";
  - the Steps lead "to a door nobody has opened yet";
  - the Rucksack was "packed the night before";
  - the Teapot is "still warm. Someone was here a moment ago."

  The Waymarks is that trail. It gives the hint of someone ahead a payoff,
  the way Journey's glyphs and a pilgrim road's cairns do. It is also the
  game's own idea: you find your way by reading marks someone left.
- **Objects:**
  - signposts, a trail map, a pocket compass, walking boots;
  - a gate left open, a plank footbridge, a lamp post;
  - the road's slow travellers: a pack donkey, a tortoise with its bedroll.
- **Shrine.** One of two:
  - **The First Cairn**, a broad cairn banded with travellers' ribbons, with
    a lamp in a niche;
  - **the Wayside Shrine**, a candle niche under a little roof on a carved
    column.
- **On the cloud-sea map.** A long grassy ridge breaks the clouds, with a
  cairn on its crown and a signpost where the golden path forks. Small
  cairns could mark the path, one per stone.
- **Title card.** Blurb "the road out of the valley". Line: "Every stone on
  the road was left for you."

### II · Beacon Hills

- **Mood.** The call to adventure, said out loud: a signal fire is lit on
  the far hill, then the next, and the chain leads away from home.
- **Sky.** `#c3c8de → #efae8a`, slate hills going to ember. Fire reads
  brightest against cool stone.
- **Why here.** This is Campbell's herald, and Journey's mountain light seen
  from the very start. It is the most direct "you are being called" of the
  six.
- **Objects:**
  - a sealed letter, a pennant, a beacon, a herald's trumpet, a watchtower;
  - the hills' messengers: a raven with a note, a fox carrying a torch, a
    hill ram, a phoenix chick.
- **Shrine.** One of two:
  - **The Great Beacon**, a stepped pyramid with a bronze fire bowl;
  - **The First Flame**, an open round temple with the fire every beacon
    was lit from.
- **On the cloud-sea map.** A steep dark peak with a fire on its summit, its
  glow warming the cloud tops. Two smaller peaks behind it could hold unlit
  beacons that catch as the chapter's stones are uncovered.
- **Title card.** Blurb "a fire on the far hill". Line: "A fire on the far
  hill, lit for you."
- **Caution.** Fire objects repeat easily, and the slate-and-ember sky is a
  cousin of Ember Steps' dusk later in the journey.

### III · Windward Downs

- **Mood.** The first wide horizon: open grass to the edge of the sky, and
  the wind going your way.
- **Sky.** `#a9d0ee → #f3f1e4`, deep blue overhead to a white haze at the
  horizon, a real clear-day gradient. Greens and browns carry the objects.
- **Why here.** This is Shadow of the Colossus's open plain and Journey's
  dunes: you step outside and the world is suddenly big. It has the most
  "vast nature" of the six.
- **Objects:**
  - a thistle, a fairy ring, a skylark;
  - a brown hare, a black-faced sheep, a wild pony;
  - a wind-bent lone tree, a shepherd's hut.
- **Shrine.** One of two:
  - **The Chalk Horse**, a white horse cut into a green hill, like
    Uffington;
  - **The Wind Tree**, an old tree on a hill where travellers tie ribbons.
- **On the cloud-sea map.** A broad, soft green island like a whale's back,
  with one wind-bent tree on top and a white chalk horse on its flank. The
  clouds around it all stream one way.
- **Title card.** Blurb "open grass and a following wind". Line: "The wind
  is going your way. Go with it."
- **Caution.** Its creatures sit close to Moss Garden's, which comes
  straight after.

### IV · The Ford

- **Mood.** The river at the edge of home: the first border you cross.
- **Sky.** `#f6ecd3 → #e1b97f`, pale gold to amber, so water blue and heron
  grey read clearly.
- **Why here.** In myth, crossing water is the first threshold. The Ford has
  the gentlest myth of the set, the ferryman who takes you over, shown with
  a hood, two small lights for eyes and a red scarf in the wind.
- **Objects:**
  - a paper boat sent on ahead, stepping stones;
  - a kingfisher on its stake, a heron, a leaping salmon;
  - a bell to call the ferry, a water wheel, a rope ferry.
- **Shrine.** One of two:
  - **The Old Bridge**, a humped stone arch with a lamp at its crown;
  - **The Ferryman**.
- **On the cloud-sea map.** Two islets with a river of cloud between them,
  an arched stone bridge over the gap, and a ferry lantern below.
- **Title card.** Blurb "the river at the edge of home". Line: "Cross the
  water, and home becomes the far shore."
- **Caution.** It is water-heavy right before The Lagoon and The Deep
  (VI, VII).

### V · The Lone Door (not built)

- **Mood.** A doorway standing by itself in open country, open, with light
  coming through from somewhere else.
- **Sky.** `#fff3d8 → #8e9ccc`, warm door-light to twilight.
- **Why here.** It is the first threshold made literal, and the most
  Monument Valley of the six.
- **Objects:**
  - a lone door on a knoll, a door in a hollow tree, a lantern arch;
  - a pair of guardian lions (the threshold guardians), a fox carrying a
    key.
- **Shrine.** The Great Door on its stair.
- **On the cloud-sea map.** A slim spire with a door frame on top, and
  light pouring through it onto the clouds.
- **Why not built.**
  - Most of its objects are frames and arches, so a chapter of them would
    repeat itself.
  - Gate, Moon Gate and Keyhole Arch already exist.
  - Its best piece would make a fine one-off in any concept, for example a
    Lone Door level on The Waymarks.

### VI · First Camp (not built)

- **Mood.** The first night away from home: a small fire, a big dark, a long
  way to go.
- **Sky.** `#2b3157 → #e2955f`, night to campfire.
- **Objects:**
  - a tent, a bedroll, a lantern, a jar of fireflies;
  - a kettle on a tripod (the one nod to Teatime worth keeping);
  - an owl, a sleeping mule.
- **On the cloud-sea map.** A low island with a tent, a campfire's glow and
  a thread of smoke rising out of the clouds.
- **Why not built.**
  - Night at chapter II breaks the day's arc, which runs from morning to
    Starfall.
  - Its sky is close to Ember Steps' Dusk.
  - A campfire already exists on page 8.

## Recommendation: I · The Waymarks

1. **It continues a story that has already started.** First Light's
   stories point down a trail. The Waymarks is that trail. "Someone walked
   here first" becomes a quiet mystery that the rest of the journey can
   keep paying off.
2. **It is the game's own metaphor.** You find your way by reading marks
   someone left, which is exactly how a nonogram is solved.
3. **It suits an early chapter best.**
   - Posts, stones, planks and boots are the simplest, most readable shapes
     of the four. The concept has the most natural openers (Signpost, Trail
     Map, Compass), at 6.5–8 D.
   - Its cool sky is the cleanest break from Dawn's peach and Mint's green.
4. **It is mythic without being loud.**
   - The First Cairn and the Wayside Shrine are humble, sacred places.
   - A ridge with a cairn and a signpost where the golden path forks is the
     most direct path image for a map built around a golden path.
   - The bigger set pieces (beacons, the ferryman, the chalk horse) are left
     for later chapters, or to borrow as single levels.

**Runner-up: Beacon Hills.** It is the strongest "call", with the boldest
colour. Choose it if chapter II should feel dramatic rather than quiet.

Picks can mix across sections: a chosen concept can take a few strong pieces
from the others. These sit naturally on The Waymarks:

- Torchbearer Fox (P10·II-07), a guide on the road;
- Ferry Bell (P10·IV-04) and Paper Boat (P10·IV-01), the brook it crosses;
- Brown Hare (P10·III-04).

### What a returning player keeps

Progress is keyed by level id: `strata.solved` in `main.js` holds
`{ time, mistakes, stars, score }` per id.

- **Moved levels keep their ids,** so their solve, stars and journal entry
  move with them. A moved level that now sits further on stays solved, but
  it does not open the trail around it. The unlock rule in `map.js
  progress()` already handles this.
- **Retired levels.** Their record stays in localStorage. Nothing deletes
  records except the developer reset. They simply stop showing:
  - the map and journal only walk the current `SHAPES` and `BONUS`;
  - "N of M uncovered" and the journal's gem total count only current
    stones.

  So a player who solved Cake Stand or the Layer Cake sees their gem total
  drop by up to 6. Nothing breaks. Both shipped today, so very few saves
  have them.
- **New chapter II stones need care.** They start unsolved. `progress()`
  counts unsolved stones as gaps unless they are in `ADDED_LATER`. Without
  that, a player who had walked past chapter II would find their trail cut
  back to chapter II, and the unsolved stones in later chapters would lock
  again. List every new chapter II level id in `ADDED_LATER`; that is the
  mechanism built on 2026-10-18 for exactly this case.
- **Shrine ids come from the chapter name.** The id is
  `bonus-${name.toLowerCase().replace(/[^a-z]+/g, '-')}`, so renaming
  Teatime changes its shrine id, for example `bonus-teatime` becomes
  `bonus-the-waymarks`. A Layer Cake solve is orphaned whatever happens.
  The new shrine starts fresh, which is right because it is a new object.
- **Some stored lists are keyed by chapter name:**
  - `strata.chapters` (title cards seen);
  - `strata.map.chapters` (awakenings played).

  The renamed chapter's card plays once for everyone, which is welcome for
  a new place. The leftover "Teatime" entries are harmless.

**Safe approach:**

1. Move rather than retire wherever a level fits, keeping ids. Retire only
   what shipped today.
2. Reserve retired ids. Never reuse `cake-stand` or `bonus-teatime` for a
   different object, or a stale save would mark the new one solved. A
   comment next to `ADDED_LATER` is enough.
3. Add every new chapter II level id to `ADDED_LATER`.
4. Give chapter II its own palette name (for example `Waymarks`) and an
   audio key in `audio.js`. Do not recolour `Teatime` in place. Once no
   level uses `Teatime`, it can stay or go.
5. Retune moved levels with `tools/retune.mjs`: `mug`, `stand-mixer`, and
   `teabag-pal` or `toaster` if needed. Curated levels (`fried-egg`,
   `bunny-teacup`, `teapot`) keep their clue masks.
6. Run `npm test`. It checks that:
   - chapter means rise;
   - each chapter ramps;
   - each shrine is harder than its chapter;
   - the legacy ids remain (it requires `mug`).

If zero loss matters more than theme, Cake Stand can stay as a First Light
level (tea at home before leaving). Only the Layer Cake would then retire.

## Notes (no changes made)

### Other chapters that feel cosy rather than epic

- **Clockwork Workshop (XI)** is the most indoor and toy-like now: a tin
  frog, tin train, tin carousel, sewing machine and microscope. As a
  *place*, it could become a great clock tower or orrery inside a
  mountain, "the machine that turns the sky". The armillary sphere,
  hourglass, pocket watch and songbird automaton already fit, and page 9's
  Clock Tower shrine alternative points the same way.
- **Moss Garden (III)** reads as a back garden: a gnome, a bench, a corgi, a
  cat. A wild "Moss Hollow" (mossy stones, small forest spirits, the Frog
  shrine) would keep its creatures and lose the fence.
- **Lantern Festival (XII)** is communal and warm. Its cast is already
  folklore, though: oni, kappa, tanuki, an origami fox, a dragon head. A
  name like "Night of Lanterns" or "Spirit Festival" would lean mythic with
  no new objects.
- **Autumn Woods (V)** is cosy (cider, chestnuts, a scarecrow), but it is
  outdoors and works as a breather. It is fine as it is.

The others already feel like places on a journey.

### First Light's name and blurb

- **Keep the name.** "First Light" already means the dawn of a journey.
- **Change the blurb.** "home and morning" frames the chapter as domestic.
  "the morning you set out" (or "home, the morning you leave it") frames it
  as a departure.
- **The card line could follow.** "Morning finds what the stone was
  keeping." is about the puzzle, not the journey. Alternatives:
  - "Everything is packed. The road is waiting."
  - "Pack light. Someone left the gate open." This one ties to The
    Waymarks' Open Gate.
- **The stones already tell the story:** a packed rucksack, a key for a lock
  down the trail, a teapot still warm. A new blurb is enough; no level
  needs to change.
