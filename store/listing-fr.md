Translated from [app-store-listing.md](app-store-listing.md), with the level count updated to 194 (the game's count after build 7). Claims and their order follow the English. Counts are measured with a script: characters for each field, and bytes (UTF-8) for Keywords, which is how App Store Connect counts them (an accented letter is two bytes). The in-game words follow the glossary in `docs/l10n/fr.md` (pierre, cube, forme, burin, tailler, marquer, faux pas, gemme, sanctuaire, monde). Tutoiement, as in the game.

French typography: the spaces before « : » and « ? » are no-break spaces (U+00A0), so App Store Connect never breaks a line before the colon. Each counts as one character. If App Store Connect shows them oddly, plain spaces are fine; the counts don't change.

The listing is for France and every French-speaking storefront (Canada, Belgium, Switzerland). App Store Connect's two French locales are **French** and **French (Canada)**; paste the same text into both.

**Strata : logimages en pierre**

(28 / 30 characters)

"Logimage" is the common French name for the nonogram (the word on French puzzle magazines and apps), so it goes in the name, where Apple weighs search words most. The home-screen label stays **Strata**.

## Description (4000)

```
Chaque pierre garde quelque chose à l’intérieur.

Strata est un jeu calme où il s’agit de le trouver. Chaque niveau est un bloc de pierre qui cache une forme : une lanterne, un renard enroulé qui dort, un phare, un dragon trop jeune pour cracher du feu. Des nombres, sur trois faces de la pierre, te disent combien de cubes de la forme se trouvent dans chaque rangée. Lis-les, taille au burin chaque cube qui n’en fait pas partie, et la forme apparaît peu à peu.

C’est un logimage en trois dimensions (un nonogramme, ou picross en 3D). Tourne la pierre entre tes mains, retire ses couches pour voir à l’intérieur, et avance rangée par rangée jusqu’à ce qu’il ne reste que la forme.

UN VOYAGE À TRAVERS QUINZE MONDES
194 pierres dans 15 mondes, des Premières lueurs et du Jardin de mousse à la Fête des lanternes, aux Abysses et à la Pluie d’étoiles. Chaque monde a sa lumière et ses couleurs, et chaque pierre que tu découvres prend sa place sur la carte, comme une petite sculpture. Dans chaque monde, un sanctuaire attend ceux qui ont gagné leurs gemmes.

FACILE À APPRENDRE
```

## Keywords (100 bytes)

```
nonogramme,logique,cube,voxel,zen,relaxant,hors ligne,nombres,cerveau,réflexion,hanjie,sculpter
```

(96 / 100 bytes; 95 characters)

Comma-separated with no spaces after the commas. Leaves out words already in the name or subtitle (Strata, logimages, pierre, casse-tête, 3D, apaisant), which Apple indexes anyway. "Nonogramme" is the other French name for the puzzle; "hanjie" is used by French puzzle sites too. "Picross" is a Nintendo trademark, so it is not a keyword; the description names it once, as a description of the genre, which is how French players search ("picross 3D"). If that line worries anyone, delete "ou picross en 3D" from the description: nothing else depends on it. "Réflexion" is the French store's word for brain games ("jeu de réflexion"); "cerveau" stands in for "brain".

## Notes for the reviewer

- **Stars → gemmes.** The English description says "stars" ("earn their stars", "never costs you a star"); the game calls them gems (◆), so this uses gemmes, the game's word, as the Japanese and Chinese listings do.
- **"A bonus stone"** is written "un sanctuaire", the map's word for it.
- **Chapters → mondes.** The English description says "chapters"; the game says worlds (Finds: "3 mondes"), so this says mondes.
- **Chapter names** in the description are the in-game ones (Premières lueurs, Jardin de mousse, Fête des lanternes, Les abysses, Pluie d’étoiles).
- **"Ceux qui ont gagné leurs gemmes"** is the one place the generic masculine appears; the game itself is written in "tu" and avoids gendered adjectives for the player.
